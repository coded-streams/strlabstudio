# Governance Model

## 1. Overview

This document defines the governance model for the Str:::lab project.  
The goal is to establish a transparent, merit-based, and community-driven structure aligned with Apache Software Foundation principles.

The project is designed to evolve under an open governance model where code, decisions, and roadmap are controlled by the community, not any single individual or organization.

---

## 2. Guiding Principles

The project follows these core principles:

- **Community over code** — healthy community growth is more important than features
- **Meritocracy** — influence is earned through contributions, not assigned
- **Transparency** — all decisions are made publicly
- **Consensus-driven development** — decisions are made through discussion and agreement
- **Vendor neutrality** — no single company or individual controls the project

---

## 3. Roles

### 3.1 Contributors
Anyone who contributes to the project in any form:
- Code contributions (pull requests)
- Documentation improvements
- Bug reports and testing
- Discussions and design input

Contributors have no voting rights but are essential to project growth.

---

### 3.2 Committers
Committers are contributors who have earned write access to the repository.

Responsibilities:
- Can merge pull requests
- Can participate in technical decision-making
- Are responsible for code quality and review

Becoming a committer:
- Based on sustained, high-quality contributions
- Nominated by existing committers
- Confirmed via lazy consensus or vote (see Section 5)

Committer status is a privilege, not a right, and can be revoked if inactive or violating project standards.

---

### 3.3 Project Management Committee (PMC)

The PMC is responsible for overall project governance.

Responsibilities:
- Define project direction and roadmap
- Ensure compliance with governance rules
- Manage releases and project health
- Onboard new committers and PMC members
- Resolve disputes and conflicts

PMC members are expected to act in the best interest of the project and community.

---

### 3.4 Project Lead (Optional / Transitional Role)

During early-stage development, a project lead may exist to coordinate efforts.

However:
- The role does not grant ownership of the project
- Authority is temporary and transitions to PMC governance
- All decisions remain community-driven

---

## 4. Decision Making

### 4.1 Lazy Consensus (Default)

Most decisions follow lazy consensus:

- A proposal is considered accepted if no objections are raised within a reasonable timeframe (typically 72 hours to 7 days depending on complexity).
- Silence is considered agreement.

---

### 4.2 Voting

For major decisions, voting may be required.

Votes:
- **+1** : Support
- **0** : Neutral / no opinion
- **-1** : Strong objection (must include technical justification)

A vote passes if:
- Majority of PMC members approve
- No binding veto remains unresolved

---

### 4.3 Veto Power

A PMC member may issue a **binding veto (-1)** only if:
- There is a serious technical, architectural, or governance concern
- The veto is accompanied by a clear explanation

Vetoes must be addressed before proceeding.

---

## 5. Becoming a Committer or PMC Member

### 5.1 Nomination Process

- Existing PMC members nominate a contributor
- Discussion occurs publicly (mailing list or GitHub discussion)
- Decision is made via vote or lazy consensus

### 5.2 Requirements

Candidates are evaluated based on:
- Quality and consistency of contributions
- Understanding of project architecture
- Collaboration with the community
- Alignment with project principles

---

## 6. Releases

- Releases are community-driven
- Must be reviewed and approved by the PMC
- Must follow versioning and changelog standards
- Must be reproducible and transparent

---

## 7. Communication

All project discussions are public.

Primary channels:
- GitHub Issues / Discussions
- Mailing list (recommended for Apache alignment)
- Optional chat channels (Slack/Discord) for informal communication

Important decisions must always be documented publicly.

---

## 8. Intellectual Property

- All contributions are licensed under Apache License 2.0 (or compatible license approved by PMC)
- Contributors retain copyright but grant usage rights to the project
- No proprietary or restricted code may be introduced
- All contributors must comply with Contributor License Agreements (CLA) if required

---

## 9. Vendor Neutrality

The project is independent of any company or organization.

- No company may dominate decision-making
- No contributor may represent external corporate interests in governance decisions
- The project is owned by its community

---

## 10. Amendments

This governance model may evolve over time.

- Changes require PMC approval
- All changes must be publicly discussed
- Updates must be versioned and documented

---

## 11. Summary

Str:::lab is a community-driven open-source project.  
Its success depends on collaboration, transparency, and merit-based contribution.

The governance model ensures that the project remains sustainable, neutral, and aligned with open-source best practices.
